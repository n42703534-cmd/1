ABSTRACT
The optimization of passenger evacuation paths in subway stations under flood scenarios plays an important role in improving evacuation efficiency and ensuring escape safety. Considering the impact of evacuation network failures such as gates on path planning, a two-stage passenger evacuation path optimization method under flood scenarios of subway stations is established with the objectives of minimizing total evacuation time, risk, and congestion. The black widow algorithm is proposed to optimize the BP neural network model to predict the travel time of passengers at nodes, which could improve the prediction accuracy of calculating the total evacuation time. The path optimization model is solved by the NSGA-II algorithm, and the optimal Pareto solution is determined based on the minimum total cost. Taking a subway station in Qingdao, China as an example, a passenger evacuation simulation system under flood scenarios is built using PathFinder software. The effect of the path optimization strategy is comprehensively evaluated through comparative experiments. It is found that the overall evacuation optimization degree could be increased by 18.75%, by comparing the evacuation time, congestion, and risk objectives under the situations with and without path optimization strategies.
1. Introduction
Urban rail transit has become an important means of transportation in recent years for citizens due to its advantages such as large volume, fast speed, environmental protection, and punctuality. A total of 290 urban rail transit lines with a total length of 10, 291 km have been opened in 55 cities in the Chinese Mainland by the end of 2022. Among them, the proportion of subway operation lines is as high as 77.85% (Association, 2022). The subway system has significantly improved the convenience of people’s travel, and the large-scale passenger flow it attracts has posed new challenges to the safe operation and management of subway stations. The statistical results of past evacuation accidents at subway stations indicate that the flood is the third most frequent type of disaster, accounting for 11% (Yu et al., 2019). Although some scholars have studied the flood evolution process in subway stations and the movement changes of pedestrians affected by floods according to our limited literature statistics (Forero-Ortiz et al., 2020; Lin et al., 2022), they did not give suggestions on the optimization of passenger emergency evacuation paths in subway stations under flood scenarios. 2. Literature review
At present, the scenario of passenger emergency evacuation in subway stations mainly focuses on emergency passenger clearance when there are safety hazards of facilities and evacuation in case of fire accidents. The research content involves evacuation capacity and risk assessment, deduction of passenger spatiotemporal distribution, simulation of passenger evacuation based on VR, optimization of internal facilities such as railings, and optimization of evacuation paths (Yang et al., 2022b). Zhang et al. (2023) analyzed the evacuation risk of tunnel fires and constructed a path optimization model based on dynamic risk values using a multiple regression model. Mandal et al. (2023) reviewed the passenger emergency evacuation in subway stations through analyzing 221 literature. Zhang and Jia (2023) studied the design method of guidance information system in subway stations to improve the efficiency of wayfinding. However, research on passenger evacuation path planning in subway stations under the typical scenario of floods is relatively limited (Goldbeck et al., 2019). The fruitful achievements made by scholars in optimizing passenger evacuation paths, analyzing the capacity of network nodes such as stairs, and simulating passenger evacuation dynamics have greatly promoted the research content of this paper. Therefore, a summary and analysis of the current research status will be conducted from these three aspects.
2.1. Passenger evacuation path optimization in subway stations
The passenger evacuation path optimization (PEPO) model in subway stations is usually constructed using the objective programming method. The objective function mainly refers to single objective and multiple objectives. Shi et al. (2012) proposed an evacuation safety strategy with the objective of minimizing evacuation time in a regular passenger clearance scenario of subway stations. Zhang et al. (2016) proposed a decision method for selecting passenger evacuation paths in subway stations under ordinary passenger clearance scenarios, with the single objective of minimizing the sum of evacuation path length, evacuation time, and passenger flow density. Yang et al. (2022d) proposed a single objective PEPO model based on Fisk stochastic user equilibrium theory, which can alleviate congestions at key locations within the subway station. There are usually conflicts between different
desired objectives of safe and efficient passenger evacuation. For example, longer evacuation time and higher passenger flow density may increase evacuation risks, and the minimum requirement for achieving both objectives simultaneously is the most ideal path decision-making solution. Overemphasizing the shortening of evacuation time may lead to potential high-density risks, thereby increasing the likelihood of safety accidents such as stampedes. Generally, PEPO can be seen as a multi-objective planning problem. At present, the relevant research mainly focuses on relatively simple scenarios, such as single-story exhibition halls, and multiple exit rooms (Shuaib, 2018; Hosseini et al., 2021; Guo and Zhang, 2022). Evacuation time, path length, congestion, risk, cost, etc. are often chosen in terms of parameter selection that affects path optimization. Guo and Zhang (2022) proposed a multi-objective PEPO model under the passenger clearance scenario of subway stations, which can improve the evacuation effect to a certain extent, especially during peak operation hours. Yang et al. (2022b) considered the auxiliary guidance role of the guide in the passenger clearance scenario of subway stations and established a dual objective programming model with the shortest evacuation time and the lowest congestion cost. The method can be used to reasonably determine the guidance path to lead the crowd to avoid the bottlenecks in advance. The above studies only considered the travel time of the road segment in the modeling process of evacuation time objective, but ignored the calculation of travel time at nodes. Nodes such as stairs and gates are bottleneck areas during the evacuation process, and congestion at the bottleneck can significantly prolong the total evacuation time, which in turn directly affects the path planning results. Therefore, the path optimization model constructed without considering node travel time is not reliable enough. Due to the complex internal structure of the subway station, the difficulty in real-time extraction of flood diffusion parameters, and the difficulty in pedestrian microscopic motion simulation of integrating flood parameters in complex scenes, research on PEPO in subway stations is rare at present, but it seems to have attracted the attention of scholars. According to our limited literature investigation, there has not been any relevant research on the PEPO in subway stations under floods. The PEPO schemes that do not consider the influence of flood parameters could have a significant impact on accurately determining the effectiveness of passenger evacuation under flood disasters, due to the significant changes in hydrodynamic parameters such as flood depth over time. Lim et al. (2013) stated that optimizing evacuation paths during floods is the key to reducing the adverse effects of floods. Therefore, one of the main tasks of this paper is to explore the path planning method for passenger evacuation in subway stations under the influence of flood parameters.
2.2. Node traffic analysis
In the evacuation network of subway stations, the traffic state at important nodes such as gates and stairs/escalators may become an important factor affecting the overall traffic efficiency. The factors that affect traffic efficiency vary at different nodes. In recent years, scholars have been conducting relevant research on passenger traffic at nodes of subway stations. The research on passenger evacuation at stairs/escalators and gates mainly focuses on building evacuation models and analyzing behavior patterns. Bao and Huo (2021) proposed a stair evacuation model that considered pedestrian rotation behavior, exploring the evacuation behavior characteristics of pedestrians under different geometric shapes of stairs. Liu et al. (2019) proposed a random FFCA model that considered the impact of visibility to simulate the evacuation behavior of people going upstairs, pointing out that reduced visibility can have a significant negative impact on the spatiotemporal distribution of pedestrians. Lei and Tai (2019) explored the impact of stair position on evacuation results through scene simulation experiments. Ye et al.

2.3. Pedestrian dynamics simulation
Conducting emergency evacuation experiments in real-life scenarios may be too dangerous, and simulation has been proven to be one of the most effective methods for studying evacuation management strategies (Zhang et al., 2019). The pedestrian motion model provides an effective mathematical modeling method for studying the evacuation behavior of passengers in subway stations, which mainly includes macroscopic models and microscopic models. Classical macroscopic models mainly include fluid dynamics models and queuing theory models (Henderson, 1974; Lovas, 1994; Lim et al., 2012). The macroscopic model describes the relationship between pedestrian flow, density, and speed, and it is not suitable for the modeling of passenger movement behavior studied in this paper. Microscopic models are based on the description of individual movement behaviors, and the accuracy of the model simulation is relatively high. The microscopic model can be divided into two categories, namely, the continuous model and the discrete model from a spatiotemporal perspective. The classical microscopic models mainly include the social force model, the cellular automata model, the lattice gas model, and the agent model. The social force model (Yang et al., 2016b,a, 2021, 2020) and the agent model (Liu et al., 2014) belong to the continuous motion model, which can simulate the movement characteristics of pedestrian flow and reproduce typical self-organization phenomena. They have been used to explore the impact of emergency evacuation signs on passenger evacuation in subway stations (Cai et al., 2022) and verify the effectiveness of evacuation plans for existing buildings (Camillen et al., 2009). The cellular automata model (Tajima and Nagatani, 2001; Yang et al., 2022c) and the lattice gas model (Guo and Huang, 2008) belong to discrete pedestrian motion models, and the direction of pedestrian motion is limited by a finite grid space. Among them, the lattice gas model is a special case of the cellular automata model. They were applied to solve the modeling problem of evacuation behaviors (You et al., 2020) .
With the gradual improvement and maturity of pedestrian dynamics models, a series of crowd dynamics simulation software have emerged, such as PathFinder, Anylogic, MassMotion, FDS+Evac, Steps, Legion, and Building exodus, which can be applied to study pedestrian evacuation in multiple scenarios (Canomoreno et al., 2021) as listed in Table 1. The crowd dynamics simulation software provides a basic simulation platform for the modeling of passenger evacuation motion in subway stations. However, different software has its own limitation and applicability. The Anylogic software simulates pedestrian movements with the social force model, but the parameters of the bottom motion model cannot be modified according to the hazard scene, and the logical expression mode of pedestrians is complex. The MassMotion software uses the minimum cost model to find the path, but it is unable to simulate the speed change of pedestrians affected by hazard parameters in the process of evacuation. The Steps, Legion, and Building exodus software are based on the discrete cellular automata model to model pedestrian movement behaviors, which are limited by the limited feasible directions within the grids, making them difficult to ensure the accuracy of crowd evacuation simulation results in complex environments. The FDS+Evac software is an open-source software specifically designed to simulate human evacuation during fires, which can directly observe changes in fire and smoke. However, the simulation modules in the FDS+Evac are not yet comprehensive, and the relevant performance of the software needs to be improved. The PathFinder software drives pedestrians based on the agent model, and can realistically simulate the individual travel habits of heterogeneous pedestrians by using the combination of path planning, guidance mechanism, and collision processing. In addition, this is also the most important thing that the software provides relevant channels to set corresponding parameters for the movement behavior changes of pedestrians affected by hazard parameters. Therefore, PathFinder software is more suitable for building passenger evacuation simulation systems under floods. At present, some achievements in understanding and simulating the evolution of hydrodynamics have been made, providing possibilities for studying the evacuation movement of passengers under floods. The depth of flood is a direct factor affecting evacuation efficiency, mainly reflected in its impact on pedestrian movement speed (Zhang et al., 2018b). In the construction of pedestrian motion velocity models in flood environments, the basic dataset of the model is mainly obtained through video analysis under the real flood event, human experiments in the laboratory environment, and simulation experiments. Dias et al. (2021) obtained the relationship between flood depth and walking speed based on the data collected in the laboratory. Lin et al. (2022) simulated the process of flood invasion through numerical simulations and analyzed the impact of different channel design structures and location distributions on personnel evacuation plans. The achievement of relevant results directly provides reliable supports for the calculation of passenger travel speed at the evacuation network under flood disasters.
3. Model
The hydrodynamic model can be used to obtain flood propulsion parameters such as flood depth and flow speed within the station, and the passenger movement speeds corresponding to different flood depths can be calculated. Then, different passenger speeds and different numbers of passengers are used as input data in the simulation scenarios of nodes such as stairs constructed by PathFinder, the simulated travel time is obtained, thereby the basic data set of the node travel time prediction model is got. It provides data support of node travel time for the calculation of total evacuation time. After that, the two-stage evacuation path optimization model is proposed considering the evacuation time, stability risk, and congestion, which is solved by the NSGA-II algorithm to obtain the complete evacuation path connecting the starting node, intermediate node, and ending node. Finally, the path optimization scheme is set at the nodes of the evacuation system constructed by Pathfinder to achieve the simulation of passenger evacuation behavior that integrates path optimization strategies.

3.1. The hydrodynamic model
There may be floods pouring into the subway station due to low terrain, unstable water retaining walls at the entrance/exit, etc., when occurring extreme weather such as rainstorms. At this time, the life safety of passengers in the station could be threatened to a certain extent, and emergency evacuation must be required. The hydrodynamic model (Zheng et al., 2019) is adopted for the calculation of flood routing in subway stations. The model can take into account the impact of different sizes of channels, stairs, platforms, etc. in subway stations on flood propulsion (Ishigaki et al., 2006). The accuracy of the simulation results is high, avoiding the risks, cost investment, and time consumption issues that exist in the process of conducting truly controllable experiments. This model has been recognized by scholars and applied to the calculation of flood propulsion in subway stations (Mo, 2010). The model is chosen for the calculation of flood parameters in subway stations because its effectiveness has been verified, and it has the advantage of high computational efficiency, which can quickly provide flood dynamics parameters for the evacuation path optimization model. 3.2. Passenger motion model
The movement modes of pedestrians in the PathFinder software mainly include SFPE mode and Steering mode. Pedestrians in SFPE mode use the length of path they are about to walk as the main reference standard and choose evacuation exits based on the principle of proximity, without considering factors such as collision and congestion. However, if the distance between individuals or the length of the path to the nearest point exceeds a certain threshold in Steering mode, a new path can be regenerated to adapt to the new environment, allowing pedestrians to deviate from the path while still moving in the correct direction. Therefore, the Steering mode of PathFinder software is more in line with the actual situation of passenger evacuation behaviors in subway stations, which is used in this paper. The motion environment of people in Steering mode is composed of a complete three-dimensional triangular mesh, and the speed of pedestrian moving to the next mesh depends on factors such as people speed, density, turning, and obstacle distribution in the forward direction. 3.3. The two-stage PEPO model
In the process of PEPO in subway stations, the physical environment of the station is usually simplified as an evacuation network diagram composed of nodes and edges. An optimization model for evacuation paths with the shortest evacuation time, the smallest congestion, and the lowest risk objectives is established, which fully considers the impact of flood evolution within subway stations on passenger escape speed and risk. It is an evacuation scenario that has not been addressed in previous evacuation path optimization studies, and it is also an exploratory study. Meanwhile, this model can be based on the path scheme allocated in the first stage, and dynamically optimize the evacuation path in the event of node or road segment failures according to the actual flood evolution situation, achieving a real-time response effect. The research content of this paper is to further enrich the optimization of passenger evacuation paths in subway stations, and provide improvement suggestions for determining evacuation paths after the occurrence of flood. The two-stage PEPO model constructed in this paper is divided into two stages. The first stage belongs to static path planning, and the main task is to comprehensively optimize the evacuation path based on the objective values of evacuation time, congestion, and risk, in order to set a reasonable evacuation plan in advance. The second stage is based on the path optimization plan generated in the first stage, and further considers the possibility of node or road segment failures caused by floods. It is necessary to optimize the evacuation path of passengers on the failed node or road segment for a second time. At this point, passengers on feasible paths still evacuate based on the path optimization results of the first stage, in order to achieve the effect of dynamically optimizing the evacuation path based on the actual flood evolution situation. The goal of the second stage optimization is to achieve the same optimization goal as the first stage in the event of node or road segment failures. Therefore, the two-stage PEPO models established in this paper are similar, except for changes in walkable nodes and road segments. Thus, only the path optimization model in the first stage is provided in this section.

After inputting the items into the prediction model, the node travel time results can be quickly obtained. The PathFinder needs to set software parameters manually one by one according to different passenger flows, which is time-consuming and labor-intensive. What is more, the software runs in 3D dynamic simulation. It takes much more time to simulate the same number of people passing through the node facilities than that of Matlab. In emergency evacuations, time is life. Therefore, a faster calculation method should be adopted from the perspective of computational efficiency requirements. Another main reason is that the proposed path optimization model involves the calculation of evacuation time objective, and the simulation run of the model is based on Matlab. It is unreasonable to set the PathFinder operation environment based on passenger flows and flood parameters to obtain the node travel time once this time is needed in the calculation process of Matlab. Considering the coordination and efficiency of the software used for model operations, PathFinder is also not applicable for calculating the node travel time which is useful for the path optimization.

4. Case study
4.1. Evacuation scenario description
A subway station in Qingdao is taken as an example to conduct simulation research on passenger evacuation under flood scenarios, as shown in Fig. 2. This station is a transfer station with two underground floors, including the platform floor and the hall floor. There are a total of 8 escalators and 3 stairs on the platform floor, 6 sets of gates, and 7 entrances/exits on the hall floor. The evacuation network corresponding to the subway station is shown in Fig. 3. Among these, the gates and stairs/escalators are topologically transformed into nodes, and the edges are formed by connecting the nodes. In Fig. 3, nodes 1 to 6 and nodes 7 to 12 represent the entrances/exits of stairs/escalators on the platform and the hall, respectively. Nodes 13 to 18 represent the gates, and nodes A1, A2, B1, B2, C, D, and E represent the exits. The total number of passengers in the initial condition is 2000 who randomly distributed on the platform, and passenger attributes are listed in Table 2 (Yang et al., 2022c). The maximum free speed of passengers is set at 1.2 m/s, and the speed would change according to flood parameters, surrounding pedestrians and obstacles. In some flood accidents, the possibility of passengers trapped in the train may be very high, for example, the extremely heavy rainstorm in Zhengzhou, China, led to the flood trapped train accident in 2021. However, passengers inside the station may be significantly affected
in some flood accident scenarios. For example, some underground platforms of subway stations in New York City were flooded during Hurricane Katrina in 2005. Although passengers can enter the station hall through the stairs, the flood blocked some passages out of the station, causing passengers to be unable to leave the platform in time, forming the dilemma of emergency evacuation. The subway station in Bangkok, Thailand was hit by severe flooding in 2011, causing some passengers to be stranded on the platform for a long time. In fact, the simulation scenario set up in this paper is only a possibility of occurrence, but the optimization method for passenger evacuation paths under floods is always effective and is not affected by the distribution of passengers in the initial state. The ultimate goal is to hope that passengers in the station can quickly and safely reach safe areas and avoid local congestions. Therefore, it is necessary to improve the efficiency and reliability of evacuation strategies for subway station managers in response to flood accidents, which also reflects the important practical significance of this study. When a flood occurs in a subway station, it may lead to potential risk accidents such as power supply equipment failures, personnel congestion and stampede accidents, causing congestion or damage to road segments or nodes and rendering them impassable. The variation of flood parameters in the case study of this paper over time at different locations within the subway station can be obtained based on the adopted hydrodynamic model, as shown in Fig. 4. In the second stage of the path optimization model constructed in this paper, it is necessary to reassign passengers on the failed road segments or nodes based on the results of the path plan made in the first stage, in order to achieve fast and safe evacuation, and avoid overcrowding at certain nodes or road segments. A robustness analysis of the evacuation efficiency of the subway evacuation network is conducted in order to determine certain key nodes. Assuming that the failure status of the key node serves as the basis for updating the evacuation network in the second stage, a path optimization plan for this scenario could be further developed. In actual evacuation scenarios, the network can be updated based on real-time conditions, thereby re-planning the evacuation path for passengers on ineffective road segments or nodes. Robustness analysis can reflect the resistance of the subway evacuation network to external interference, characterizing the ability of the network to maintain normal operation after a certain road segment or node is attacked and fails. The sudden flood is viewed as a random attack on the evacuation network, and the global network efficiency is selected to measure the traffic capacity of the evacuation network. With the rapid development of computer technology, the use of mobile applications and applets has become very popular. 5. Conclusions
A two-stage evacuation path optimization model for passengers under flood scenarios of subway stations is constructed in this paper, taking into account the total evacuation time, the congestion, and the risk values. The model can provide evacuation plans for passengers in case of ineffective road segments, which is more in line with actual evacuation requirements. The black widow algorithm is used to optimize the BP neural network model for predicting the travel time of passengers at key nodes, which can improve the calculation accuracy of evacuation time. The relationship between the evacuation time objective, congestion objective, and risk objective, as well as the impact of the weights of each objective on the path plan, are analyzed based on the objective results of the path optimization model. The optimization effect of the model is further explained by comparing the evacuation results with and without path strategies. The results

The PathFinder software is used to construct a three-dimensional evacuation simulation system of a subway station in Qingdao, China and the evacuation behaviors of passengers under flood scenarios are explored. The simulation results show that: (1) The cumulative increase in flood depth over time could let more passengers choose nearby intermediate nodes in the path optimization plan. Meanwhile, the optimized plan also considers the spatio-temporal balance of passenger flow to avoid an excessive use of a particular node. (2) The slope of the fitting line between the predicted results of the travel time prediction model at nodes such as gates and stairs/escalators and the true values can be as high as 0.9932 and the error of the prediction model, such as MSE, can be as low as 0.6718, indicating that the prediction performance of the model is relatively good. (3) The three objective values of evacuation time, congestion, and risk interact with each other. The evacuation time objective is positively correlated with the congestion objective, while the risk objective is negatively correlated with the evacuation time objective. (4) The path optimization strategy can significantly improve the evacuation efficiency of passengers after network failures, and the use of paths and exits is more reasonable. The total evacuation time has been reduced by 69 s, and the overall optimization degree can be improved by up to 18.75% through using NSGA-II for solving. The research results of this paper do have some limitations. On the one hand, the parameters in the model may be uncertain, which could affect the generation of path optimization results. Rapid dynamic programming for handling unexpected uncertain events is crucial. The research content of this paper is a preliminary exploration of modeling the optimization of passenger evacuation paths in subway stations under flood scenarios. One of our future research directions is to use optimization modeling methods such as stochastic optimization and robust optimization to address parameter uncertainty in solving evacuation path optimization problems. On the other hand, the rapid release and effective transmission of path strategies is an important link in ensuring the implementation of optimization solutions. Developing intelligent path navigation functions embedded in mobile applications or applets is also an important research topic in the future.
